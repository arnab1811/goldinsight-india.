How to interpret the results

A higher EUR/INR is better for converting euros. A lower INR gold price is better for buying jewellery. You can lock in a favorable exchange rate and hold INR until gold reaches your target. These comparisons assume the same conversion fee, unchanged making charges and tax, and known hypothetical future prices. They do not predict the best timing.

How the numbers are calculated

Future Indian 22K price = current Indian benchmark × (1 + global USD gold change) × (1 + USD/INR change). This assumes the local duty, premium and purity structure scales proportionally and stays unchanged. It is a sensitivity model, not an empirical forecast.

Future EUR/INR = current EUR/INR × (1 + chosen currency change). INR received = euro amount × exchange rate × (1 − conversion cost percentage). The future euro cost of jewellery equals its INR invoice divided by the applicable net exchange rate.

Break-even: with unchanged percentage charges, waiting for both is cheaper in euros if the proportional EUR/INR increase exceeds the proportional Indian gold price increase. If converting now and buying later, only the Indian price change affects the comparison (ignoring interest and holding costs).

GST and making charges are editable simplified assumptions; actual invoice structure, fixed fees, local premiums and taxes may vary. The dated scenario chart remains illustrative and merely rescales to the chosen base; it is not updated by macroeconomic forecasts.

Can this identify the best buying month?

No. The paths are judgment-based illustrations, not econometric estimates or probability intervals. January–February is a review window, not a forecast of the actual minimum. A rate decision can move prices either way.
